Clients Aren't Built to be Launchers!

The competitive PvP community kept using 1.8 well after Mojang stopped updating it.

To keep it stable, supported, and optimised, a fair bit of work went into 1.8 client modifications.

This need combined with a desire for client security (for anticheats) and uniformity (for allowlists).

Many 2016 competitive PvP servers made their own clients.

Soon, these clients realised how profitable having cosmetics, adverts, and partnerships could be.

Most of these clients outgrew their founding servers and became standalone products.

These clients then spent tens of millions of dollars buying each other into consolidated ecosystems.

These PvP products pivoted, switching to trying to monetize MC's general userbase.

1.8 (and the value of these clients) is not relevant outside of PvP! The modern ecosystem is unique!

The community's modern modding efforts stem from a fundamentally different base from these clients.

The current modern support of these clients is just a grafting of modern features from elsewhere.

The modern 'optimizations' of PvP clients are repackagings of community work supported elsewhere.

These client groups self-perpetuate: large monetized userbases are profitable, which funds marketing.

The reason these clients are still widespread is due to that marketing, not their inherent value.

Even the main value-add of these launchers (cosmetics/friends) can be found elsewhere for free.

Everything PvP clients repackage is better used directly within its original ecosystems.

PvP products are painful to support.

They are scrambled: we can't see inside them.

  • PvP clients are closed source and obfuscated.
  • When something breaks, we don't know why.
  • Finding out why takes hours of reverse-engineering.

That scramble changes every update.

  • Obfuscation renames everything to random letters.
  • For Lunar in particular, these random letters change.
  • If we target something, it will break constantly.

These clients quietly change what we build on.

  • Normally, we build on MC's shared public foundation.
  • These swap parts of that out with private scrambled copies.
  • Stuff attached to the original quietly does nothing.
🌯

The community's loader is Fabric: Lunar's isn't.

  • Lunar runs mods on something that looks like Fabric.
  • It's actually their old core wrapped around Fabric.
  • Things quietly behave differently than we expect.

These clients 'optimize' by bundling others' work.

  • Most 'optimizations' in PvP clients are modified FOSS.
  • We know public mods, not privately changed copies.
  • Original authors can't help debug unknown changes.

These clients ship ancient 'official' preset packs.

  • Wynn and its mods update often; these clients don't.
  • The version of wynn mods packaged is dozens behind.
  • Old versions result in old bugs long since fixed.
